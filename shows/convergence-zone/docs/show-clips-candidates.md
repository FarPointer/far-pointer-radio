# Candidate spoken-word clips for Convergence Zone

**Date:** 2026-08-08 · **Branch:** `copilot/collect-cool-radio-clips`

A shortlist of short spoken-word moments — about radio, listening, sound, and space — that
could work as drops, bumpers, or intros for Convergence Zone. Every candidate carries three
things it cannot air without: **who said it**, **whether the words can legally be broadcast**,
and **whether the person creates reputational risk for the station**.

Companion document: [`show-clips-speaker-vetting.md`](show-clips-speaker-vetting.md) — the
per-person due diligence behind the vetting column here.

This is a *candidate list*, not a cleared list. Nothing here has been rights-cleared, and no
audio has been sourced or edited. Treat every row as a proposal awaiting a human decision.

## The two screens every candidate has to pass

### 1. FCC screen

Convergence Zone airs **Tuesday at 8:30 PM PT on 90.7 KSER-FM and 89.9 KXIR-FM** — inside the
**6 a.m.–10 p.m.** window in which the FCC prohibits indecent and profane material. The
10 p.m.–6 a.m. "safe harbor" does not apply to this show, so **nothing on this list may rely
on it**. Obscene material is prohibited at all hours.

The FCC publishes no list of banned words; enforcement is complaint-driven and contextual,
weighing explicitness, repetition, and whether the material appears to shock or titillate.
Source: [FCC — Broadcast of Obscenity, Indecency, and
Profanity](https://www.fcc.gov/enforcement/areas/broadcast-obscenity-indecency-profanity).

Risk ratings used below:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CLEAN** | No profanity, sexual, or excretory content in the words themselves. Airable as written. |
| **REVIEW** | No obvious violation, but something in the clip or its surrounding audio needs a listen before air — a nearby line, an ambiguous word, or a source recording that has not been heard end to end. |
| **DO NOT AIR** | Contains material that is plainly at risk outside safe harbor. Listed only so nobody rediscovers it and proposes it later. |

The ratings cover the **quoted words**. The *recording* still has to be auditioned in full —
a clean sentence can sit two seconds away from an unusable one, and this list was built from
transcripts, not tape.

The FCC rules bind the over-the-air signal. The stream, the Mixcloud archive, and social
clips are not FCC-regulated, but station policy and simple judgement still apply, and a drop
built for the show will end up in all four places.

### 2. Speaker screen

Everyone on this list was researched for documented history of abuse, sexual misconduct,
relationships with minors, domestic violence, racism or antisemitism, and extremist
affiliation. Verdicts: **CLEAR** (no credible reports found), **CAUTION** (something a
decision-maker should know), **FLAG** (substantiated or admitted conduct — do not air without
a deliberate editorial decision).

Airing a voice is an endorsement of a kind, and a listener-supported station wears it. The
detail, the sourcing, and the allegation-versus-finding distinction are in
[`show-clips-speaker-vetting.md`](show-clips-speaker-vetting.md). Read it before choosing.

## Rights, which is the other half of the problem

The FCC screen and the speaker screen are about whether the station *should* air something.
Neither says anything about whether it *may*.

- **NASA mission audio and the Golden Record** are US Government works and are the cleanest
  material on this list. Start here.
- **Commercial audiobooks, documentaries, and interviews** (Sagan, Watts, Le Guin's speech,
  most broadcast archive) are licensed, and short-excerpt fair use is an argument, not a
  permission. A nonprofit station's tolerance for that argument is a decision for station
  leadership, not for a producer at the board.
- **Voice and likeness** survive death in Washington State. Estates — Wolfman Jack's, Watts's,
  Sagan's — actively license.
- **Quotations from books** (McLuhan, Eno's liner notes) are text. Reading them on air is a
  different and far safer act than airing a recording of the author, and it removes the audio
  rights problem entirely.
