I was already contemplating death and resurrection as I produced this week’s episode of Convergence Zone - and then my friend Nick texted me the sad news that Ryuichi Sakamoto had passed away.

Sakamoto-san’s impact — from his time in Yellow Magic Orchestra, his work in film scores and acting, and his prolific creations as an ambient and neoclassical artist — is impossible to do justice in words, at least as few as I can summon here. But I can try and share some of his recent pieces, including one produced in part during his time recording as Bastyr University in Kenmore, WA.

His final album, “12”, dropped in January, consisting of solo sketches that Sakamoto-san recorded in his home over the past few years on piano and electronic instruments. In several, you can hear him breathe as he begins to play.

Hearing Ryuichi Sakamoto breathe and create — on a quiet and cool early Sunday, on the day I was stunned by his death — was painful and exquisite. I am so grateful for what he left us all.

On this week’s Convergence Zone, several tracks by Ryuichi Sakamoto from “12” and “\[async\]”; a tribute to his theme from “Merry Christmas Mr Lawrence” by Electric Youth; and a piece from Vancouver artist Locsil featuring samples from piano works composed to perform with Sakamoto-san.

Also: new music from Dreamstate Logic, Craig Padilla & Marvin Allen; recent music from Terminus Void and from Arushi Jain

Let's begin with the minimalist composer Philip Glass -- in 1983, he wrote an opera based on the life of the Egyptian pharaoh Akhenaten. The tenth ruler of the Eighteenth Dynasty, he was a cultural and religious iconoclast -- he abandoned most of the Ancient Egyptian pantheon, focusing the state on worship of a single god, the sun-disc Aten. His royal name, in fact, translates to "Effective for the Aten". He built the power of his state around the Aten, even building a new capitol city and leaving the traditional center of state and religious power, Thebes. After his death, Egyptian life flowed back to the traditional religions, and the state took great efforts to destroy Akhenaten's city and erase him from history - his story was lost entirely until the discover of the ruins of his city in the late 19th century.

The opera was commissioned by the Wurtemburg State Opera in Germany - it premiered in 1984, with the Stuttgart State Opera conducted by Dennis Russel Davies. We're listening now to the Prelude from Act 1 -- let's listen to it now, on Convergence Zone - 90.7 KSER
